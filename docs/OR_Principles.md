I actually think this is one of the most important documents in the project. It isn't about code—it's about preserving the identity of Radio Outdoors. Here's how I'd write it today.

---

# Radio Outdoors Principles

## Version 1.0

*"Get Out. Set Up. Make Contact."*

---

# Our Mission

Radio Outdoors exists to encourage amateur radio operators to leave the shack and enjoy operating from the outdoors.

Whether that means a national park, county park, campground, trail, boat, scenic overlook, cabin, backyard, or anywhere else under the open sky, every outdoor operating experience has a story worth sharing.

Radio Outdoors celebrates those adventures.

---

# Our Vision

The purpose of Radio Outdoors is not to build another logging application.

The purpose is to build a community of outdoor amateur radio operators who inspire one another through shared experiences.

People return because of the stories.

The contacts are simply part of the adventure.

---

# Core Philosophy

Outdoor operating comes first.

The experience comes first.

The adventure comes first.

Statistics, awards, and contacts are important, but they are never the primary purpose of the site.

---

# What Radio Outdoors Is

Radio Outdoors is:

* An outdoor operating community
* An adventure journal
* A travel journal
* A place to inspire others
* A place to discover new operating locations
* A collection of real operating experiences

---

# What Radio Outdoors Is Not

Radio Outdoors is not:

* Another logging program
* A replacement for Logbook of The World
* A replacement for QRZ logging
* A replacement for Parks on the Air
* A contest logger
* A database built around statistics

Those tools already exist.

Radio Outdoors complements them.

# Adventures

Everything begins with an Adventure.

Members do not create posts.

Members do not create activations.

Members...

## Start the Adventure.

An Adventure may last:

* One hour
* One afternoon
* One weekend
* Several weeks

There is no required length.

---

# Journal Entries

Every Adventure is built from Journal Entries.

Journal Entries tell the story.

They may include:

* What happened
* Photos
* Wildlife
* Weather
* Equipment changes
* Great contacts
* Problems encountered
* Lessons learned

Journal Entries should feel natural.

Not like filling out a form.

---

# Stories Matter

A single memorable contact can be more interesting than 200 routine contacts.

The Journal should answer:

What made today memorable?

---

# Photos Matter

Photos help people imagine themselves there.

Photos of:

* The operating position
* The scenery
* Wildlife
* Friends
* Campgrounds
* Boats
* Trails
* Sunsets

are often more valuable than equipment photos.

---

# Equipment

Equipment is important.

Equipment is not the story.

Simple descriptions are preferred.

---

# Results

Operating results belong near the end of an Adventure.

Examples:

* Contacts
* States
* Countries
* Parks
* Modes
* Bands

Interesting...

But never the focus.

---

# Keep It Simple

Every feature should answer one question:

Does this make it easier to enjoy amateur radio outdoors?

If the answer is no...

Don't build it.

---

# Publish Quickly

Starting an Adventure should take less than one minute.

Everything else can be added later.

People should never feel overwhelmed.

---

# Explore Adventures

# Design Philosophy

The website should inspire people to go outdoors.

Pages should feel:

* Clean
* Modern
* Bright
* Friendly
* Adventure focused

Never cluttered.

Never database driven.

Never intimidating.

---

# Development Philosophy

Build first.

Discuss second.

Freeze completed work.

Move forward.

Don't redesign finished pages without a compelling reason.

Perfect should never prevent progress.

---

# One Guiding Question

Every design decision should answer one question:

> **Will this encourage someone to get outside, set up a radio, make contacts, and share the adventure?**

If the answer is yes...

Build it.

If not...

Don't.

## Final Thought

Radio Outdoors isn't really about radios.

It's about **the memories made because someone decided to take a radio outdoors.**

That is the difference.

And I think that's what people will remember.

Radio Outdoors Design Principles
1. Adventures come first.

Everything exists to tell the story of an outdoor radio adventure.

The radio is part of the adventure—not the adventure itself.

2. Simplicity wins.

If a feature requires an explanation, it probably needs to be redesigned.

Operators should spend their time outdoors, not filling out forms.

3. Capture memories, not just data.

Logs record contacts.

Radio Outdoors records experiences.

Photos, journal entries, locations, and stories are just as important as the QSOs.

4. Every Adventure is a story.

Every adventure begins with Start the Adventure.

Every journal entry becomes another chapter.

Years later, an operator should be able to relive the day.

5. Operators, not users.

Throughout the application:

Operator
Adventure
Journal
Story
Station

Never generic software terminology unless it's behind the scenes.

6. Outdoor first.

Every screen should encourage getting outside.

Not collecting points.

Not chasing awards.

Getting outside.

7. The application stays out of the way.

The software should disappear behind the experience.

The best compliment would be:

"I forgot I was entering data."

8. The first version stays focused.

Version 1.0 doesn't need every idea.

If a feature doesn't make it easier to tell the story of an adventure, it can wait.

9. Community through stories.

People connect through experiences.

Comments, photos, and journals build community better than statistics alone.

10. Preserve the memories.

One day, an operator—or their family—should be able to look back through years of adventures.

Not to count contacts.
