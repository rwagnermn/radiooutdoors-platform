The story behind the contacts

Radio Outdoors™ preserves the stories behind outdoor amateur radio Adventures.

It gives each person a lasting place to share those stories with family, friends, and future generations.

It is not just a logbook.

A logbook records callsigns, bands, modes, dates, and signal reports. Those details matter, but they do not explain why the day mattered.

It is not Facebook.

Facebook is useful for conversation, planning, and sharing a link. Radio Outdoors is the permanent home of the Adventure, not another endless social feed.

It is not POTA.

Radio Outdoors does not tell you where you are allowed to sit or require a predefined park. Any suitable outdoor place can become part of the story.

It is a mashup that tells the whole story.

Radio Outdoors brings together the useful parts of a logbook, public sharing, maps, photographs, and outdoor operating records. The result is not record keeping for its own sake. It is a story about where you went, who went with you, what you tried, what worked, what failed, and what you will remember.

The contacts support the story. The Adventure is the reason the story exists.

Built to outlive the moment

Photos get buried in phones. Social-media posts disappear into a feed. Logbooks preserve contacts but rarely preserve the people, weather, mistakes, humor, and memories around them.

Radio Outdoors is intended to keep those stories available long after the trip is over—and, hopefully, long after the storyteller has taken the dirt nap.

Create the story here. Share the link everywhere else.

Public Adventures can be viewed by anyone without creating an account. Send the link by email, text, a club newsletter, or social media. Friends and family can read the story without joining anything.
